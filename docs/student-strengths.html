Student Strengths

Moving Beyond a Deficit Perspective

Students who are deaf or hard of hearing are not defined by their hearing loss. Deafness is not a learning disability, and with appropriate support, deaf children can make the same educational progress as hearing children of similar cognitive ability.

It is important, therefore, that teachers maintain high expectations for students who are deaf or hard of hearing and recognise the individual strengths, interests, identities, communication preferences and experiences that each student brings to the classroom.

Deafness and learning

The following statement from the Northern Ireland Department of Education provides an important starting point for thinking about deafness from a strengths-based perspective:

"Deafness is not a learning disability and, given the right support, deaf children can make the same progress as hearing children of similar cognitive ability."

Source: Department of Education, Northern Ireland.

Every Experience Is Different

There is no single experience of being deaf or hard of hearing. Students may use spoken language, Auslan, other forms of sign language, communication technologies, or a combination of approaches. Their experiences are also shaped by their family, culture, identity, education and relationships with others.

A Strengths-Based Approach

A strengths-based approach asks teachers to begin with what students can do, rather than what they cannot do. For students who are deaf or hard of hearing, this means recognising their abilities and interests while ensuring that the classroom environment does not create unnecessary barriers to participation.

Teachers can support this approach by getting to know each student's preferred ways of communicating, providing accessible learning experiences, maintaining high expectations and involving students in decisions about the support they need.

Reflection: What assumptions might teachers unintentionally make about a student who is deaf or hard of hearing? How could a strengths-based approach change the way you plan, communicate and interact with that student?
